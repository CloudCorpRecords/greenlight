The Agent Policy Index

How do open-source projects treat AI agent contributors? Every entry below comes from real submissions made in October 2026: issues claimed, pull requests opened, bots and policies met face to face. This is the map we wish had existed before we started.

45 repos indexed

How to read the classes

Agent contributions bannedThe repo's agent policy forbids autonomous agent contributions and AI-drafted PR text. Do not submit there with an agent.
AI disclosure requiredAgents may contribute, but the repo requires AI involvement to be disclosed in titles, bodies, and release notes.
Assignment requiredYou must be assigned the issue by a maintainer before work counts. Unassigned PRs may be auto-closed.
Claim firstComment on the issue to claim it before you start. Silent starts lose races.
Triage holdIssues in triage are not ready to build on. Wait until the triage labels clear.
Outside PRs pausedThe repo has structurally paused or auto-closes external pull requests. Do not target it.
Maintainer-only PRsExternal PRs are not accepted. The contribution is a detailed, verified issue comment.
Collaborators-only PRsOnly collaborators can open PRs. Outside fork-based PRs are rejected or never land.
Vouch requiredA maintainer must vouch for you (or you must join a contributor list) before your PR survives.
CLA requiredA Contributor License Agreement signature is required before merge. Find the exact path, it is fragmented across repos.
DCO sign-off requiredEvery commit needs a Signed-off-by trailer. Unsigned commits fail the gate.
Discussion firstOpen an issue or discussion and get agreement before you build, especially for new features.
Bot-gated intakeA bot triages incoming work with labels like needs-product-decision. Claims are fine; PRs wait for a human signal.
Open to agent-assisted PRsNo structural gate found against agent-assisted contributions. Standard contribution rules still apply.

A repo can carry several classes. Evidence under each card tells you exactly what proves the class: the policy file, the workflow, or what happened to our PR. When a class is our inference rather than a documented rule, the evidence says so.

Method

Each entry traces to one of three sources: the campaign's repo intelligence notes (39 repos touched between October 8 and October 17, 2026), direct policy discoveries from the contribution wave (policy files, workflows, and discussion threads read in full), and Greenlight's automated friction measurements. Entries carry the date they were verified, because policies change. This page is maintained alongside the campaign: when a repo's policy changes, its entry gets a new verified date.

The raw dataset is published for machines at policy.json (CC-BY-4.0). Greenlight's own gates implement this taxonomy: run npx @reneturcios/greenlight check <issue-url> and the agent policy of the repo is checked before you invest a weekend.
